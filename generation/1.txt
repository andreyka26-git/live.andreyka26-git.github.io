## Introduction

I have been working for Microsoft for 4 years and already wrote [an article](https://live.andreyka26.com/i-left-microsoft-after-4-years) about why I decided to leave as well as [an article](https://live.andreyka26.com/i-got-an-offer-at-google-and-snowflake-in-2025) about Google and Snowflake offers.

This article will be about the relocation process from Prague (Czech Republic) to Dublin (Ireland) as a Software Engineer hired by Snowflake. Besides that I will cover the details, pros/cons about living in both of these countries that might help you to choose the one.


## Relocation Process

One important point: since russia has invaded Ukraine I didn’t finish my work permit properly for the Czech Republic and had to switch to a Special Temporary Protection visa for Ukrainians. \
The problem with it is that your experience does not count towards residence permit / citizenship - therefore I didn’t really see a stable future in the country. There is no way to switch to a work permit inside the Czech Republic.

In 2024 I called the Irish and Netherland’s embassy in the Czech Republic to ask whether it is even possible to issue a work permit in case I find an employer. I remember the Netherlands said yes - just find an employer, and Ireland said: “no, for ukrainians we do not issue any work permits, there is not even a button on the system for that, you just go with passport”. (`Spoiler - you actually CAN do that, I’m stating that as an IRP and Critical Skills visa owner`).

When I got an offer from Snowflake - due to the above information, I wanted to make sure that I would get a proper visa at that time, before I do anything.

How it works in Ireland:



* you apply for a **Critical Skills Employment Permit** (**CSEP**, stamp 1 visa). This gives you right to work for specific employer for 2 years (generally)
* **Irish Residence Permit (IRP)** - this is issued for one year generally, and it gives you the right to stay in the country.

It sounds to me a bit confusing, but yes these are 2 different docs and you obtain them separately as well. To get IRP you need to be in Ireland and have Critical Skills + bunch of other docs.


### Critical Skills Visa Application

Snowflake, as most of the big tech companies, hire third party partners that do visa applications for employees. In autumn 2025 they asked for a bunch of docs and I uploaded everything that was required from my side under their supervision.

In 1-2 months (end of Autumn) I got the Critical Skills visa, so I could start the relocation.


### Ireland - here we go


![alt_text](images/image1.png "image_tooltip")


Snowflake provided initial money for hotel / temporary accommodation and flight tickets. I booked the closest to the office hotel, and in January I was in Dublin. \
 \
**Critical Skills** != **Residence permit** - so how can you enter the country then? At the airport they put a stamp in the passport saying that you have 3 months stay in Ireland for getting IRP. To be precise, you just have to book an appointment within 90 days. The appointment itself can be done later. Note though, the appointment registration (online) is possible only within Ireland.

I felt a bit of pressure, and my first goal was to get IRP. But it requires other docs, including address confirmation (lease agreement with landlord \ bills with your name). So I scheduled an IRP appointment within the first week, because the queue can be very long, delaying it by 2-3 weeks.


### Home finding

You’ve probably heard that the home situation in Dublin is crazy. For me it took 2-3 weeks to find an apartment, given: I was single, no pets, no kids, no need for parking, and could live in very small studios without much comfort. \
 \
I had probably 5-7 viewings, and during every single one I saw 10-30 people going for the same apartment. Potential tenants were sending documents right away asking to pick them.

Maybe I was lucky, but I got a very small studio (12-14 m^2) with shared washing machines for 1600 eur monthly. Not bad quality of apartment and 20 mins walking distance to the work, which was amazing for me. One downgrade - shared washing machines are paid, so every cycle costs me something like 5-6 eur, and sometimes the meters are full so you literally cannot dry or wash until the landlord empties it.


### PPSN (Personal Public Service Number)

This is sort of a tax number or social number. Without it reported to the employer your salary is taxed 52% (emergency tax). So you want to have this one as soon as possible.

For PPSN you need:



* Passport
* Address confirmation (lease agreement \ bills with your name)
* Employment contract

I have heard rumors that it is possible to apply with a temporary address confirmation doc, for example issued by hotel letterhead. But I was not able to check that because my PPSN appointment was rescheduled and hotel confirmation had already expired by that time. So I used my long term lease.

Don’t forget to report your PPSN to employer’s payroll and Revenue myAccount so that you are not emergency taxed.

I got my PPSN in January, and it was my first very pleasant surprise about Ireland. As slavic person who lived in Ukraine and Czech Republic - I usually expect a very bad attitude and bad mood on the day of going to any government institution. Usually people there are pretty rude, unhappy and sometimes just hate you. But Ireland - it is a completely different case. I was happy returning from the place I got PPSN, people were very kind, smiling, explaining extra stuff, asking if I need a translation, etc. This is a completely different experience, and I’m very grateful for that.


### IRP (Irish Residence Permit)

I was lucky again because I got my appointment in February. Nothing special - just prep all the docs (lease agreement, employment confirmation, passport, insurance) and went for an appointment. I remember I didn't actually need all the docs I brought, it was just me panicking, as I had only 3 months for that.

Now I could exhale, I had a place to live, and docs to be / work legally here. It felt nice by that point.

The next step for most engineers here is to live like that for 21 months and apply for a stamp 4 visa which is sort of “permanent residence” that makes you independent of your employer, as for now  I can only work for Snowflake and no one else.

Then in 3 years (5 in total) - apply for citizenship. However right now there are moves to make it 8 years, which is okay, for me it does not change much - I loved the country and didn’t plan to move out anyway.


## Healthcare

Maybe some of you remember my experience with failed appendicitis surgery in Prague back then. I was very picky, careful and anxious about healthcare.

As I understood, Ireland has a hybrid approach: private healthcare / insurance, and public one.


### Public healthcare

Under some net income you are eligible for a Medical Card which makes pretty much everything healthcare related free. It is not a static rate it depends on your expenses as well, but roughly it means 2k eur NET a month.

With that card you will have free GP visits, tests, and specialist visits along with emergencies.


### Private healthcare

For income above 2k eur net a month you are paying for most of the stuff.

There are a bunch of private hospitals with specialists as well as clinics with GPs. How it usually works: you have insurance that pays some parts of expenses for you. However, for specialist appointments you generally need to go to a GP. \
I guess, this is because Europe in general has huge problems with healthcare budgeting.

Therefore queues to specialists and labs might be shorter for you compared to public ones.

Note though, that with private insurance - I **almost never was** fully paid back, sometimes it is 50% of coverage, sometimes it’s 80%, very rarely it is 100%. Something is not paid at all, e.g. vaccination renewal, some blood tests, etc even though it was referred by GP.


### Emergency

I am so glad I didn’t have to check, but my understanding is that the emergency room and service is flat rate (100 eur) for everything you might need to save your life (or get an appendectomy xD). If you need to call the ambulance - it can be a bit more just to get you there.


### My experience with healthcare

The first thing is how GPs work here. I was very confused, as some people said that there **IS** an assigned GP and I need to find and get assigned. Then a bunch of clinics and GPs told me that they don’t have the concept of “assigned” one.

My understanding for now is that if you have private insurance, and you don’t have an assigned one - you go to whatever clinic and GP you want and pay for that. Then in case any specialist asks you - you can refer to your GP you visited last.

 \
Snowflake provides health and dental private insurance (VHI) for employees. However it usually does not cover everything as I mentioned. GP visits are covered something like 60-70%, physicotherapist appointments are covered 50%, however dental (hygiene) was fully covered and I didn't even need to fill in the claim.

The problem is that a lot of times you need to add a claim manually, check whether it is approved and if not ask for more docs from the medical institution. One time I didn’t get my ultrasound covered, another time, same hospital - it was covered fully. My vaccination renewal was not covered at all (e.g. tetanus every 10-15 years). However it was ALL referred to by GP.

 \
Worth mentioning one more time - I’m so pleasantly surprised by how kind and nice people are here, making sure you understood everything, not angry at you whatsoever.

For now I have an amazing GP, very intelligent and knowledgeable (which is apparently rare nowadays xD), and a bunch of private and public clinics to go to if something happens. So I’m pretty happy with the situation.


## Taxes

Painful topic. For all big tech enjoyers - congrats, your stocks are taxed on vesting (52%) + taxed on profit gain (33%). If you invest into an ETF - you have “Unrealised gains taxed” that taxes you every 8 years even if you don’t sell it.

For the salary taxation - it is progressive.


## Properties

In my opinion it is much better than in Europe in case you want to stay here. Why so? Yes, rent prices are extremely huge in Ireland, BUT if you decide to buy an apartment, most of the time your mortgage is CHEAPER than renting (excluding initial payment)

So in case you want to stay here for more than a few years - it makes sense to just go for a mortgage and save a lot on rent. Worth noting, the apartment prices in Dublin and Prague are very close to each other, however the salaries are not.


## Czech Republic vs Ireland

Given I spent four years in Prague and already almost one year in Dublin I have some things to compare. Disclaimer: it is SOLELY my opinion.

Please don’t perceive it as any hate or something. I am deeply thankful to all European and non-European countries that made temporary protection, let me and other Ukrainians work and stay there. From my side I always tried to be a good resident, be grateful, never committed crimes, always paid taxes, etc.

This information is only for the people who are considering relocation to Czech Republic vs Ireland and want to see some opinions.


### Language

In the Czech Republic you will need Czech for a comfortable life. Working for Microsoft does not require Czech and most of my colleagues didn’t know and didn’t learn Czech. However, any time you need to go to a Czech point, post office, healthcare institution, government institution - believe me you will struggle a lot if you don’t speak any slavic language. Besides that your  mental state will degrade a lot, cause usually you are getting hated much more for not speaking Czech.

Ireland has English spoken everywhere.

Personally, I didn’t learn Czech, as I didn't know whether I wanted to stay long term or not. Since I already had b2 English level - Ireland was an amazing choice from that standpoint.


### People

This is my observation, but I believe that slavic people are a bit more rude (including me and ukrainian people), and a bit more nationalists, and in terms of migration policy - they are more right wing. I saw some evidence of it throughout my life.

For Ireland it is much more chill, I felt a huge difference and in general having people more talkative, nice and kind. I love that, and I want to be such a person as well (working on changing my “slavic face” expression to something that has at least some signs of a smile.)


### Healthcare

In general EU healthcare is very similar. In CZ they had more centralized semi public semi private insurance called VZP, and generally speaking you don’t have to deal with claims, it is autopaid mostly, and if it is not - it is just not - you gonna pay by cash.

I don’t think there is a huge quality difference. There are always bad doctors and good doctors, and you try to find good doctors. No real punishment for mistakes (negligence, just not following protocols, etc) made by doctors (I have heard a lot of stories). Unfortunately this is the underbudgeting issue that I hope humanity will take into account.

It shouldn’t be the case that I care about feature rollout more than doctors about doing some procedure or prescribing medications, but it is, unfortunately.


### Taxes

The Czech Republic is the clear winner here. First of all it has just lower taxes for both salaries AND stocks. Apart from that CZ has a rule that if you hold vested stock for more than 3-4 years - you can sell without paying capital gain tax. However the amount of stocks you will get in CZ compared to Ireland is dramatically different - go and check levels.fyi for MS for example.


### Software Engineering Market

Clear winner by Ireland. There are so many more bigger companies, almost the whole FAANG abbreviation, and tier 2 companies, whereas the big tech companies representation in Prague is only Microsoft (I mean big dev centers, not just sales offices for 50 people). There are still a bunch of tier2 companies, like Jetbrains, Pure Storage, etc, but still Ireland has so much more.

### Naturalization

Prague requires 10 years + Czech Language + historical/cultural questions. Ireland (as of now) requires 5 years, no tests for now except “Good character” verification (convictions incl. abroad, driving offences, court cases, cautions).

However, there was an [amendment proposed](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-justice-home-affairs-and-migration/press-releases/government-approval-secured-for-the-priority-drafting-of-the-irish-nationality-and-citizenship-amendment-bill-2026/) that will add language exams at least (potentially Irish language as well) and extend the residence time required from 5 to 8 years. Which is still better than in the Czech Republic.
